01 / Computational Modelling

Computational Fluid Dynamics of an Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm

A computational investigation of haemodynamic behaviour within an idealised abdominal aortic aneurysm using ANSYS Fluent.

ANSYS Fluent CFD Haemodynamics

01

Overview

How do different inlet boundary conditions affect haemodynamics within an abdominal aortic aneurysm?

This project used Computational Fluid Dynamics to investigate blood flow through an idealised abdominal aortic aneurysm under steady and pulsatile inlet conditions.

Three inlet conditions were compared: a steady uniform profile, a fully developed parabolic profile and a transient periodic waveform representing pulsatile flow. The resulting velocity fields, recirculation regions and wall shear stress distributions were then analysed to examine how both the aneurysm geometry and inlet condition influenced the haemodynamic environment.

02

Engineering Approach

Geometry & Mesh

Idealised AAA Model

The three-dimensional model represented a symmetric abdominal aortic aneurysm with a 20 mm inlet and outlet diameter, expanding to approximately 60 mm through the aneurysm.

The computational domain contained approximately 166,000 predominantly structured hexahedral cells.

Fluid & Solver

Blood Flow Model

Blood was modelled as an incompressible Newtonian fluid under laminar flow conditions using a pressure-based solver in ANSYS Fluent.

Density: 1050 kg/m³
Dynamic viscosity: 0.0035 Pa·s

Flow Field

Geometry-driven changes in the velocity field

The enlarged aneurysm geometry produces a distinct high-velocity central flow region surrounded by substantially lower velocities within the aneurysm sac.

03

Boundary Conditions

01 / STEADY

Uniform inlet

A constant inlet velocity of 0.0565 m/s was applied uniformly across the inlet surface, providing a simple baseline against which the developed and transient inlet conditions could be compared.

02 / DEVELOPED FLOW

Parabolic inlet

The uniform condition was replaced with a fully developed parabolic velocity profile implemented using a compiled User Defined Function. Velocity was greatest at the vessel centreline and reduced towards zero at the wall, producing a more developed inlet flow field.

03 / TRANSIENT

Pulsatile inlet

For the transient simulation, the steady inlet condition was replaced by a periodic waveform implemented using a Fourier-series-based UDF generated from Scotti flow data. Multiple cardiac cycles were simulated using a 0.05 s timestep to establish periodic flow behaviour.

04

Results

Velocity & Recirculation

Central jet formation and aneurysmal recirculation

Across the simulations, the expansion of the vessel produced a characteristic high-velocity central jet while substantially lower velocities developed closer to the aneurysm wall.

Vector plots revealed local flow separation and recirculation within the enlarged aneurysm sac. Although much of the flow remained concentrated within the central jet, surrounding fluid formed vortical regions as the vessel expanded.

Transient Flow

Flow structures evolve throughout the cardiac cycle

The transient simulation produced a continuously changing flow field rather than the fixed structures observed in the steady simulations. At higher inlet velocities, a stronger central jet developed through the aneurysm with recirculation occurring closer to the vessel walls.

During lower-flow phases, the central jet weakened and recirculating structures became more dominant within the aneurysm sac. CFD-Post visualisation showed these vortical structures developing and moving through the geometry as the inlet velocity changed.

Local Velocity Analysis

Pulsatile behaviour varies throughout the geometry

Velocity was monitored at the inlet, outlet, aneurysm centre and within the aneurysm bulge over successive cardiac cycles. The resulting histories demonstrate how the imposed pulsatile behaviour propagates through the model.

The inlet and outlet responded rapidly to changes in the pulsatile waveform, while velocity within the aneurysm bulge showed a more delayed and damped response. This behaviour corresponded with the recirculating flow structures present within the enlarged region.

Wall Shear Stress

Wall loading changes with geometry and flow phase

Wall shear stress was greatest near the narrower inlet and outlet regions where near-wall velocity gradients were higher. Within the expanded aneurysm sac, lower wall shear stress developed alongside the low-velocity and recirculating flow regions.

Under transient conditions, wall shear stress also varied throughout the cardiac cycle. Negative x-direction wall shear stress identified regions of reversed near-wall flow associated with recirculation within the aneurysm.

Key Finding

Increasing the realism of the inlet condition revealed increasingly dynamic haemodynamic behaviour.

The uniform inlet provided a useful baseline, while the parabolic profile produced a more concentrated centreline jet and stronger near-wall velocity gradients. The transient periodic simulation provided the most physically realistic approximation in this study, capturing time-dependent changes in velocity, vortex structure and wall shear stress throughout the cardiac cycle.
